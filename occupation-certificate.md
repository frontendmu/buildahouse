# After Completion

The house is finished, but the project is not. These steps make it legally occupiable, insurable, and sellable.

## 1. Occupation Certificate

Under the **Building Control Act**, you must obtain an **Occupation Certificate** from your local authority before occupying the building. Building without one, or occupying without one, is a breach — and it will surface later when you try to sell, refinance, or insure.

**How it generally works:**
- Apply to the local authority (the same one that issued your BLP) once construction is complete.
- The local authority inspects the building against the **approved plans and the BLP conditions**.
- Certificates or clearances from other authorities may be required — commonly Fire Services, and confirmation of utility connections.
- The certificate is issued if the building complies.

⚠️ **[VERIFY]** — Confirm the current application form, document list, fees, and processing time with your local authority, and check the official BLP/Building Control guidance on the [Local Government Portal](http://la.govmu.org).

**This is where unauthorised changes come back to bite you.** If the house as built differs from the approved plans — an extra room, a covered terrace, a different setback — the certificate can be refused until it is regularised. Regularising afterwards is far harder than getting a variation approved during the build. See [[permit]] and [[contractor]].

- [ ] House as built matches the approved plans, or every deviation was formally approved during construction
- [ ] Application submitted to the local authority
- [ ] Required clearances obtained
- [ ] Inspection passed and Occupation Certificate received
- [ ] **Original kept safely — you will need it to sell, refinance, or insure**

## 2. Utility Connections

Most utilities require the BLP, and some require the Occupation Certificate.

- [ ] CEB electricity connected — see [[CEB]]
- [ ] CWA water connected — see [[CWA]]
- [ ] Wastewater: sewer connected or septic tank commissioned — see [[WMA]]
- [ ] Internet and telephone installed — see [[telecom]]

## 3. Municipal / District Council Records

- [ ] Notify the local authority that the building is complete so the property records are updated
- [ ] Confirm whether any **general rate** or municipal tax applies to your property, and how it is assessed. **[VERIFY]** current rules with your local authority — obligations differ between municipal and district council areas, and between residential and commercial use.
- [ ] Confirm the arrangements for household **refuse collection**
- [ ] Confirm your official **address / house number** is registered

## 4. Insurance

- [ ] The contractor's construction insurance has ended — make sure your own cover starts **the day it does**, with no gap
- [ ] Home / fire insurance in place, at a sum insured that reflects the rebuild cost, not the land value
- [ ] If you have a loan, confirm the policy satisfies the bank's requirements — see [[finance]]
- [ ] Contents insurance if wanted

## 5. Claim Your Refunds and Grants

Do not leave money unclaimed. Several schemes are claimed **after** completion and have deadlines.

- [ ] VAT refund on construction of a residential building applied for — see [[MRA]]
- [ ] Home loan refund / repayment scheme claimed if eligible
- [ ] Roof slab or building materials grant finalised if applicable
- [ ] All invoices, receipts, and the contractor's VAT invoices kept — **these claims are refused without documentary proof**

⚠️ Scheme conditions, ceilings, and deadlines change with each annual Budget. Confirm what currently applies with the MRA before assuming eligibility.

## 6. Keep Your Project File

Keep everything together, and keep a digital backup. A future buyer, bank, or insurer will ask for it.

- [ ] Title deed
- [ ] Survey plan
- [ ] Approved architectural and structural drawings, including as-built revisions
- [ ] BLP and all clearances
- [ ] Occupation Certificate
- [ ] Contract, all variation orders, and all payment receipts
- [ ] Bill of Quantities and final account — see [[quantity-surveyor]]
- [ ] Site photographs, especially of reinforcement, conduits, and pipework before covering — see [[construction-phases]]
- [ ] Warranties, guarantees, and appliance manuals
- [ ] Utility connection references and account numbers
- [ ] Insurance policies
- [ ] Tax and refund claim records

## 7. The Defects Liability Period

For the agreed period after handover — commonly 6 to 12 months — the contractor must fix defects at their own cost.

- [ ] Snag list from [[construction-phases]] cleared
- [ ] Report new defects **in writing** as soon as they appear, before the period expires
- [ ] Watch for what only shows up over time: leaks in the first heavy rain, cracks in the first few months, damp patches, doors and windows sticking
- [ ] **Retention released only after the period ends and all defects are fixed** — see [[contractor]]

## ⚠️ Important Note

🚨 **Do not release the retention early because the contractor asks nicely.** The first cyclone season and the first heavy rains are the real test of the roof, the waterproofing, and the drainage. Wait them out.
