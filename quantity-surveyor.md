# Costing and the Quantity Surveyor

An architect designs the house. A **quantity surveyor (QS)** tells you what it will actually cost to build, and checks that what you are billed matches what was built.

## 1. Do You Need a QS?

For a modest single-storey house, many people rely on their architect's estimate and comparable contractor quotations. For anything larger, or if you are borrowing significantly, a QS pays for themselves.

A QS will:
- Produce a **cost estimate** early, so the design can be adjusted before it is too late
- Prepare a **Bill of Quantities (BOQ)** — an itemised measure of every element of work
- **Evaluate tenders** — spot the abnormally low bid and the missing item
- **Value work in progress** for each payment stage
- **Price variations** so you are not at the contractor's mercy on extras
- Prepare the **final account**

## 2. The Bill of Quantities Is Your Main Cost Control

A BOQ lists each item of work with its measured quantity and a rate: cubic metres of concrete, square metres of blockwork, kilograms of steel, square metres of plaster.

Why it matters:
- **Every contractor prices the same quantities**, so quotations become genuinely comparable — see [[contractor]].
- **Variations get priced at agreed rates** already in the BOQ, instead of whatever the contractor asks once you are committed.
- **Progress payments can be measured** rather than argued about.

Without a BOQ, you are negotiating every extra from a position of no information.

## 3. Cost Estimation

Construction cost in Mauritius is commonly discussed as a **rate per square foot of built area**, varying widely with the level of finish — structure only, standard finish, or high-end.

⚠️ **[VERIFY] — Current Rs/ft² benchmarks are deliberately not stated here, and I was unable to find an authoritative published source for them.** There is no official Mauritian body publishing residential construction rates per square foot; the figures circulating online are anecdotal and age quickly. Material prices also move with import costs and the exchange rate, so a stale figure here would mislead someone's entire budget. Get current rates from:
- Two or three quantity surveyors or architects, for your specific finish level
- Recent itemised quotations from contractors on your own plans
- People who have completed a comparable house **in the last 12 months**

When someone quotes you a rate per square foot, always ask: **what does it include?** A rate that excludes finishes, boundary wall, and utilities is not comparable to one that includes them.

## 4. Where Budgets Break

- **Finishes.** Tiles, sanitary ware, kitchen, doors, and windows have an enormous price range and are chosen late, when enthusiasm is high and discipline is low. Set a finishes budget early and hold it.
- **Variations.** Small changes during the build, each individually reasonable. See [[contractor]] on written variation orders.
- **Forgotten externals.** Boundary wall, gate, driveway, water tank and pump, landscaping. See [[finance]].
- **Over-specified structure.** As noted in [[Architect]], unnecessarily high-end structural materials inflate cost without improving the house.
- **Material price movement** on a long build.

## 5. Buying Materials Yourself

Some owners supply some materials directly to save on the contractor's margin. Weigh it honestly:

**Can work well for:** tiles, sanitary ware, doors, windows, paint, light fittings — items you choose anyway and that are easy to count.

**Rarely worth it for:** concrete, steel, blocks, cement — contractors buy these at trade prices you will not match, and if you supply them, any shortage, wastage, or delay becomes your problem and your excuse-in-waiting for the contractor.

If you do supply materials, make the split explicit item by item in the contract, and agree who bears wastage.

## 6. Checklist

- [ ] Cost estimate obtained **before** the design is finalised
- [ ] Design adjusted to the budget, not the reverse
- [ ] BOQ prepared (or an itemised specification if no QS is engaged)
- [ ] All contractors priced on the same BOQ
- [ ] Finishes budget set, item by item, with a ceiling
- [ ] Externals costed: boundary wall, gate, driveway, tank, pump, landscaping
- [ ] Utility connection costs included — see [[CEB]], [[CWA]], [[WMA]]
- [ ] Contingency of 10–15% held separately — see [[finance]]
- [ ] Owner-supplied vs contractor-supplied materials split written into the contract
- [ ] Agreed rates in place for pricing variations
