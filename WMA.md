# WMA — Wastewater Management Authority

How your house disposes of wastewater depends on whether a **public sewer** is available in your area. Find this out early — it changes both your plans and your budget.

## 1. First Question: Is There a Sewer in Your Street?

Ask the **Wastewater Management Authority (WMA)** whether the public sewer network is available at your plot.

- **If yes** — you apply to connect to the sewer, and no septic tank is needed.
- **If no** — you must provide **on-site disposal**: typically a septic tank with an absorption pit or soakaway.

Your architect must know the answer before finalising the plans, because the tank and pit need space, minimum distances from the house and boundaries, and suitable ground.

## 2. Connecting to the Public Sewer

Documents commonly required for a new sewer connection application:

- National Identity Card of the applicant
- Copy of the Title Deed of the property
- Site plan / location plan
- Building and Land Use Permit — see [[permit]]
- Application form and applicable fee

⚠️ **[VERIFY]** — Exact document list, current fees, and the application form should be confirmed against the WMA's own published requirements before you apply. Contact the WMA or check the official WMA pages on the government portal.

## 3. On-Site Disposal — Septic Tank and Absorption Pit

Where no sewer exists:

- The **septic tank and absorption pit must be shown on the drawings** submitted with your BLP application — see [[permit]].
- Sizing depends on the number of occupants and the ground's absorption capacity.
- **Percolation of the ground matters.** Rocky or poorly absorbing ground may require a larger pit or an alternative system. This is related to, but not the same as, the structural soil test in [[land]].
- Minimum separation distances apply — from the house, from boundaries, and critically from any borehole or water source. Confirm the current requirements with the local authority and WMA.
- Confirm explicitly whether the septic tank and pit are **inside or outside** your contractor's quoted scope — see [[contractor]]. This is a frequently excluded item.

## 4. Plan for Emptying

A septic tank needs periodic emptying by tanker. Make sure a tanker can physically reach the tank access point — this is easy to design out by accident and expensive to fix afterwards.

## 5. Checklist

- [ ] WMA asked whether public sewer is available at the plot
- [ ] Answer given to the architect **before** plans are finalised
- [ ] If sewer available: connection application submitted with BLP and title deed
- [ ] If no sewer: septic tank and absorption pit sized and shown on the BLP drawings
- [ ] Ground absorption suitability confirmed
- [ ] Separation distances from house, boundaries, and any water source confirmed
- [ ] Tanker access to the tank possible
- [ ] Septic tank and pit confirmed as included or excluded in the contractor's scope
- [ ] Cost included in the project budget — see [[finance]]
